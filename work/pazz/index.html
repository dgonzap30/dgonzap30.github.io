Engineering detail: what's working, what's still in progress

Shared domain rules and recoverable document handoffs keep operating decisions consistent.

What's working

  • A quoting flow that turns a vehicle spec into a real-cost estimate — rent, estimated ISR deduction, creditable IVA — compared across terms
  • Claudio, a guided-search adviser that carries a confirmed budget into vehicle questions
  • A partner-application intake, gated behind a feature flag rather than live

Still in progress

  • Every amount is a local illustrative estimate, not a lessor offer — the product itself labels it "Estimación, no oferta"
  • Claudio runs on a deterministic scripted mock (USE_REAL_AGENT_MODEL off); no live model call and no autonomous decision
  • The partner application sits behind USE_PARTNER_PORTAL_V2, off by default while the partner program stays intentionally dark
  • The vehicle catalog itself isn't shown here: its cards currently render an "Imagen en validación" placeholder because licensed vehicle media doesn't exist yet
01 / Problem

One customer goal, many operating systems.

The customer sees a simple goal: find a vehicle and understand whether leasing it makes sense. Behind that goal are vehicle data, pricing and tax rules, eligibility, documents, partners, external systems, and human review.

Improving one screen in isolation would not solve the system. Catalog data must be usable. Quotes must apply the same rules everywhere. Prequalification must preserve context. Operators need to know what happened, what failed, and what to do next.

Status boundaryIn-development replacement platform. This page describes role and decisions, not private implementation.

03 / Key decisions

Selected engineering decisions.

The work treats the marketplace as one operating path rather than a collection of disconnected interfaces.

  • Centralize quote and eligibility semantics instead of duplicating them across customer, partner, and operator surfaces.
  • Preserve context through multi-step journeys so re-entry does not restart the process or discard prior answers.
  • Model handoffs as owned states with explicit inputs, outputs, and recovery behavior.
  • Keep models inside bounded workflows rather than allowing them to become silent business authority.
  • Treat timeouts and partial failures as expected operating states, not exceptional afterthoughts.
  • Separate source, CI, runtime, and production evidence, then bind release claims to the artifact actually verified.
Design principleA failed handoff should return to an understandable operating state, not disappear between systems.
