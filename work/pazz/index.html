  • A quoting flow that turns a vehicle spec into a real-cost estimate — rent, estimated ISR deduction, creditable IVA — compared across terms

  • A partner-application intake, gated behind a feature flag rather than live

Still in progress

  • Every amount is a local illustrative estimate, not a lessor offer — the product itself labels it "Estimación, no oferta"
  • Claudio runs on a deterministic scripted mock (USE_REAL_AGENT_MODEL off); no live model call and no autonomous decision
  • The partner application sits behind USE_PARTNER_PORTAL_V2, off by default while the partner program stays intentionally dark
  • The vehicle catalog itself isn't shown here: its cards currently render an "Imagen en validación" placeholder because licensed vehicle media doesn't exist yet

One goal, many systems

The customer's goal is simple: find a vehicle and understand whether leasing it makes sense. Behind it sit vehicle data, pricing and tax rules, eligibility, documents, partners, outside systems and human review.

Improving one screen would not fix that. Quotes have to apply the same rules everywhere, prequalification has to keep its context, and operators need to see what happened, what failed and what to do next.

What I do

I set technical direction and stay hands-on in the implementation, across product, engineering, operations and outside constraints:

  • Product architecture and delivery sequencing
  • Customer web and mobile experiences
  • Vehicle discovery, quoting and prequalification
  • Conversational intake and bounded applied-AI workflows
  • Partner, operator, document and contracting handoffs
  • Shared domain models, integrations, observability and release gates
Authorship This describes my role and contribution to a shared product, not sole authorship.

Decisions

  • Keep quote and eligibility rules in one place instead of copying them across customer, partner and operator surfaces.
  • Carry context through multi-step journeys, so coming back doesn't restart the process or lose earlier answers.
  • Model each handoff as an owned state with explicit inputs, outputs and recovery.
  • Keep models inside bounded workflows, never as silent business authority.
  • Treat timeouts and partial failures as normal operating states, not afterthoughts.
  • Keep source, CI, runtime and production evidence separate, and tie every release claim to the artifact that was actually verified.
